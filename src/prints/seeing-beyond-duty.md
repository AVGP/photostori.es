Maybe we are built like that path.

Somewhere early on we are handed a direction, a patch of ground to keep clear, a list of things that are ours to carry, and we call the carrying duty and the direction life. It is not wrong. Something has to be carried. Duty is how a day keeps its shape.

But look at the path in the picture. It runs downhill, into the shade, covered in fallen leaves, the shadows stretched along it. It is the most walked part of the forest and the least lit. It is also the only part of the picture a person walking it would ever see. That is the strange property of a duty: the longer you walk inside it, the more it looks like the whole forest. The eye settles at the height of the next step. The shadows become the floor. We stop asking what is casting them.

Nobody tells you that the light is behind you and above you at the same time. That the sun has been in the frame the whole time, up in the branches, and the leaves at your feet are only bright because of it. That to see it you do not need to leave the path at all. You need only to do the one thing the path never asks for, which is to stop and lift your head.

It costs a second. It can feel like disobedience, or something outside the norm, or even a small revolution. But since when did noticing become a revolutionary act?

And yet the leaves at your feet are only gold because of what you are not looking at.

There is a small hut at the left edge of the picture, half in shadow, easy to miss. Someone built it there, in the forest, off the path. We never learn who, or whether they were happy. We only know that one person once decided the walking was not the whole of it, and left a roof behind as proof.

The picture does not ask us to leave the path. It asks something smaller and harder. To remember, while carrying what is ours, that what lights the path was never on it. And that duty, for all it asks, never once asks you to look up.

So look up. Then keep walking. The path will still be there, but you will know what is lighting it.
