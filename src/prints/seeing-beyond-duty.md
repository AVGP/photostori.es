Maybe we are built like that path.

Early on we are handed a direction and a list of things to carry, and we call the carrying duty and the direction life. It is not wrong. Something has to be carried. Duty is how a day keeps its shape.

Look at the path. It is the most walked part of the forest and the least lit. Walk it long enough and it starts to look like the whole forest. The shadows become the floor.

Nobody tells you the light is behind you and above you. The sun has been in the frame the whole time, and the leaves at your feet are only gold because of it. You do not need to leave the path to see it. You only need to do the one thing it never asks for: stop, and lift your head.

It costs a second. It can feel like disobedience. But since when did noticing become a revolutionary act?

There is a small hut at the left edge, off the path. Someone once decided the walking was not the whole of it, and left a roof behind as proof.

Duty never once asks you to look up. So look up. Then keep walking. The path will still be there. Now you know what is lighting it.
